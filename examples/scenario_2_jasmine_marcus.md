## Activity 1 — Intro Slide Deck (5 min)

- Give her a "job": holding the vocabulary card and reading the term aloud when teacher cues it. [Strength: helping roles]

### Marcus
- Hand him printed slide notes so he isn't trying to write while listening. [Present Level: Written Expression below grade level]

**Ready-to-use vocabulary card (Jasmine):**

> **Moral** = about right and wrong
> **Narrative** = a story

> **Manifestation** = a sign of something

**Watch-for (Jasmine):** If she is silent and not tracking the slides, give her the verbal cue job immediately — re-engages her before she disengages further. [Present Level: avoidance is silent withdrawal]

### Section B — Partner Reading, Paragraphs 3–7 (the hardest stretch)

**Jasmine:**
- Pre-flag paragraphs 3, 5, 7 with sticky tabs — these contain the definition and the Newcastle example.
- Partner reads aloud TO her; Jasmine does not have to read aloud unless she volunteers. [Present Level: Informational Text Comprehension Gr 2]
- For DRQ-2A ("How does Lowe's example of belonging to the Newcastle community support his definition?"), give her the simplified version below. [Goal 3: Comprehension 50%→75%]
- For DRQ-2B (sharing her own community), this is a **strength moment** — verbal, personal, no reading required. Let her shine here. [Strength: peer conversation]

**Marcus:**
- Partner MUST read aloud — written into his pairing. Marcus listens, then discusses. [Present Level: Reading Fluency]
- For DRQ-2A, allow him to dictate his answer to his partner OR type one sentence rather than handwrite. [Present Level: Written Expression]
- For DRQ-2B, this is verbal — full grade-level participation expected. [Critical gap: verbal at grade level]

**Ready-to-use scaffolded DRQ-2A (Jasmine):**
> Lowe says a community is "a group of people who share a story."
> Lowe's story is about ____________ (what city?).
> He shows he is part of this community because he ____________ (name one thing he does).
> This supports his definition because ____________.

**Watch-for (Marcus):** Slumping or long pause = he's lost the thread of the read-aloud. Quietly ask his partner to back up one paragraph. Don't ask Marcus directly if he's lost — he won't say. [Marcus profile: doesn't ask for help, slumps and pauses]

**Watch-for (Jasmine):** Multiple bathroom requests in this section = comprehension overload. Offer the scheduled break NOW and re-enter on DRQ-2B (the verbal strength question). [Accom: Scheduled breaks; Goal 1: Self-regulation]

## Synthesis

### Overlapping needs (one prep serves both)
- **Read-aloud of all printed materials** (slide notes, MC stems, SR-1 prompt) — Jasmine needs it for comprehension (Gr 2 informational), Marcus needs it for fluency. One read-aloud, two students served.
- **The SR-1 graphic organizer** — same template, different function. Jasmine uses it to access the text; Marcus uses it to externalize thought he already has. Print two copies.
- **Partner read modality for paragraphs 3–7** — both students benefit from hearing rather than silent-reading the hardest text section.
- **Word/vocabulary pre-teaching** — supports Jasmine directly (Gr 3 vocab) and supports Marcus indirectly (so he doesn't burn working memory decoding).

### Divergent needs (different scaffolds at the same activity)
- **DRQ-2A:** Jasmine needs a comprehension scaffold (fill-in version of the question); Marcus needs an output scaffold (allow oral or typed answer). Same question, opposite ends of the bottleneck.
- **SR-1 target output:** Jasmine target is 3–4 sentences with 1:1 support; Marcus target is a full paragraph independently with tools. Don't apply Jasmine's reduced expectation to Marcus — it underestimates him.
- **MC questions:** Jasmine needs evidence access (sticky tabs, re-read); Marcus needs decoding bypass (audio). The MC questions themselves are not too hard for either — the access route differs.
- **Discussion (Activity 4):** Jasmine needs nothing — this is her strength. Marcus needs a recording partner so his verbal contribution isn't lost. Watch the asymmetry.

### Interaction effects (opportunities created by this pair)
- **Pair them as discussion partners for DISC-1/2/3.** Both have strong verbal skills, both struggle with output. They will produce richer talk together than with random partners — and you can assess Goal 3 (Jasmine) and verbal-comprehension evidence (Marcus) in one observation.
- **Use Jasmine's verbal strength as a model for Marcus.** When Jasmine answers DRQ-1A or DISC-2 aloud well, it gives Marcus a verbal anchor that he can build a written response from.
- **Watch the timing of avoidance signals.** Both students go silent under stress (Jasmine: head down, bathroom; Marcus: slump, pause). Your check-in schedule (after each transition) catches both with one routine. Build it once, use it three times.
- **Caution:** Don't seat them so close that one student's withdrawal cues the other's. If Jasmine puts her head down and Marcus is next to her, Marcus may interpret it as permission to disengage. Adjacent but not touching.
