## Activity 2 — During Reading Questions (15 min)

### Section A — Whole Class, Paragraphs 1–2

**Modifications:**
- Before reading paragraph 1, point Jasmine to the starred sentence in paragraph 9 and tell her: "This is the answer we're working toward. Keep your eye on it." This anchors comprehension up front. [Present Level: Info Text Comp Gr 2]
- For **DRQ-1A** ("What claim does Lowe make about the word 'community' in paragraph 1?"), use **DRQ-1B [SUPPORT]** with Jasmine even though it's optional — but break it into smaller verbal pieces (see ready-to-use below). [Accom: small group / scaffolded support; Goal 3: literal & inferential comprehension]
- For **DRQ-1C** ("summarize at least three key traits of a community"), have Jasmine give the answer **verbally first**, then write only one trait on her organizer. [Present Level: strengths — verbal participation; Writing below grade level]

**Ready-to-use scaffold for DRQ-1A/1B:**

> **Scaffolded version of DRQ-1A:**
> "In paragraph 1, Lowe says the word community has a strange ______ to it. He says it makes us feel ______ and ______." (Answers: power; togetherness; positivity)
>
> **Scaffolded version of DRQ-1B:**
> "Paragraph 1 tells us community is a powerful word. The bulleted list in paragraph 2 tells us what a good ______ of community has to do." (Answer: definition)

**Ready-to-use Central Idea Organizer (give before reading):**

| What is a community? (write in your own words) |
|---|
| _____________________________________ |

| 3 traits of a community (from paragraph 2) |
|---|
| 1. ______________________________________ |
| 2. ______________________________________ |
| 3. ______________________________________ |

**Watch-for:** If Jasmine puts her head down during whole-class reading, this is her primary avoidance signal — frustration is already high. Quietly hand her the break pass and say, "Take 3 minutes, then come back to question 1C with me." [Goal 1: Self-regulation; Behavioral pattern]

### Section B — Partner Reading, Paragraphs 3–7

**Modifications:**
- This is a high-risk section: 5 paragraphs of independent partner reading at Grade 7 level when Jasmine reads informational text at Grade 2. Pre-read paragraphs 5–6 (the Newcastle example) **with her 1:1 for 60 seconds** before partner work begins, summarizing in plain language: "Lowe lives in Newcastle. He says he's part of that community because he chose to make Newcastle's story part of his own story." [Present Level: Info Text Comp Gr 2; Accom: 1:1 check-ins]
- For **DRQ-2A** ("How does Lowe's example of belonging to the Newcastle community support his definition?"), give Jasmine the sentence frame below so she can contribute to her partner verbally and write the frame. [Goal 3: inferential comprehension; Accom: Graphic organizers]
- For **DRQ-2B** ("Consider the communities you are a part of…"), this plays directly to her strength. Let her speak first; she will likely thrive here. [Present Level: strengths — peer conversation]

**Ready-to-use sentence frame for DRQ-2A:**

> Lowe's definition says a community is *a group of people who share an identity-forming narrative*. His Newcastle example supports this because he chose to ____________________ and he feels ____________________ when people say bad things about the city. This shows that being part of a community is about ____________________.

**Watch-for:** If she asks for the bathroom pass during partner work — especially a second time — this is the avoidance signal escalating. Intercept: "Come check in with me first, then you can go." Use the check-in to assess if the partner pairing is working or if she needs the scaffolded summary repeated. [Behavioral pattern; Accom: Reminders to pause/plan/proceed]

### Section C — Whole Class, Paragraphs 8–11

**Modifications:**
- For **DRQ-3A** ("Why does the author include another bulleted list in this section?"), this is a high-inference question. Reframe verbally for Jasmine: "Look at the list in paragraph 2 — it was the *rules* a definition has to follow. Look at the list in paragraph 8 — is Lowe checking those rules?" [Goal 3: inferential 50%→75%; Accom: Repeat directions]
- For **DRQ-3B** ("explain at least three reasons…"), reduce the requirement to **one reason in writing + two reasons verbally** during share-out. [Present Level: Writing below grade level, 50% accuracy independently]
- For **DRQ-3C** (highlight two pieces of evidence in paragraphs 10–11), this is a "find evidence" task and is more accessible. Limit her search to paragraph 11 only and require **one** piece of evidence. [Accom: Graphic organizers/scaffolds]

**Ready-to-use scaffold for DRQ-3B:**

> "Lowe's definition works because…" (pick ONE to write, then say two more out loud to your partner)
> ☐ It explains all kinds of communities — both places (like Newcastle) and groups (like a religion).
> ☐ It explains why we feel proud or hurt about our community.
> ☐ It explains why community is different from just "society" or "family."

**Watch-for:** If she's quiet during whole-class share-out for Section C, **call on her with a question you've already practiced 1:1** (e.g., the Section A check-in answer). Cold-calling a new question = head down. Calling on a rehearsed answer = confidence boost. [Present Level: strengths — specific positive praise; Behavioral pattern]

## Activity 3 — Independent Practice (20 min)

### Multiple Choice (5 min)

**Modifications:**
- Pre-printed strategy checklist at top of page: *"Read question → underline key word → cross out 2 wrong answers → pick best."* [Accom: Checklists]
- Schedule a 1:1 check-in at the start of MC. Do MC-1 **together** as a model so the pattern is set. [Accom: 1:1 check-ins]
- For **MC-3** ("slighted"), this is a vocabulary-in-context question — Jasmine's vocab is at Gr 3, so this item is the most accessible. Flag it as her "anchor" question to start with for confidence. [Present Level: Vocab Gr 3]
- Allow extra time — she does not need to finish all four to move on. Priority order: MC-3 → MC-1 → MC-4 → MC-2. [Accom: Extra time]

**Ready-to-use modeled think-aloud for MC-1:**

> "The question asks the *main purpose*. Main = biggest. The whole article is trying to do ONE main thing. Lowe spent the whole article giving us a definition (paragraph 3 and paragraph 9 both say 'a community is…'). So the answer is D — to give a clear and useful definition of community."

---

### Short Response — SR-1 (15 min)

**Modifications:**
- This is the highest-risk task in the lesson: 15 minutes of independent extended writing at 50% independent accuracy. Provide the sentence-frame scaffold below at the start — do not wait for her to struggle. [Accom: Graphic organizers; Present Level: Writing — performs at grade level with 1:1 support]
- Set a **scheduled break at the 5-minute mark** regardless of how she looks. Pre-empt the head-down signal. [Accom: Scheduled breaks; Goal 1]
- Reduce vocabulary requirement: instead of "incorporate relevant unit vocabulary," require **one** of the four bolded words (aspect / moral / narrative / specific) — and pre-circle "narrative" as the easiest fit since it appears in the prompt itself. [Present Level: Writing below grade level]
- The student self-checklist should be read aloud with her once before she begins. [Accom: Repeat directions]

**Ready-to-use SR-1 scaffold:**

> **Prompt (in plain language):** What does Lowe mean when he says a community is "a group of people who share an identity-forming narrative"?
>
> **Paragraph frame:**
> When Lowe says a community is "a group of people who share an identity-forming **narrative**," he means that a community is made of people who share the same ____________________ (story / history / way of seeing the world).
>
> One detail from the text that shows this is when Lowe writes about ____________________ (Newcastle / the Mafia / his own community).
>
> Another detail is when he says ____________________ (quote or paraphrase from paragraph 9 or 11).
>
> This matters because community ____________________.
>
> **Self-check before turning in:**
> ☐ Did I answer what Lowe means?
> ☐ Did I include at least two details from the text?
> ☐ Did I use the word "narrative" (or aspect / moral / specific)?

**Watch-for:** Two signals to watch for in this 15-minute block — (1) head down on desk, (2) second bathroom request. **Either one** = pause her, hand her the break pass, and on return restart with the sentence frame, not from scratch. Specific praise for any progress: "Jasmine, the first sentence you wrote uses 'narrative' exactly the way Lowe does — that's the work right there." [Goal 1; Behavioral pattern; Present Level: strengths — specific positive praise]

## Activity 4 — Student-Led Discussion (5 min)

**Modifications:**
- This activity plays to Jasmine's strongest channel: verbal peer conversation. Minimal scaffolding needed. [Present Level: strengths — verbal participation, peer conversation]
- For the recording requirement ("Both partners record their answers"), allow Jasmine to **record her partner's answer in 1–2 words** rather than full sentences. [Present Level: Writing below grade level]
- Start with **DISC-1** (concrete, personal — "courteous behavior in your community") — this is her best entry point. If time runs short, skip DISC-2 (most abstract). [Goal 3: literal before inferential]

**Ready-to-use prompt support for DISC-1:**

> "A community I'm part of is ____________________. In that community, a courteous (polite) thing to do is ____________________."

**Ready-to-use prompt support for DISC-2 (if reached):**

> "Do you think you can be in a community without agreeing with everything? ☐ Yes ☐ No
> Because ____________________."

**Watch-for:** End-of-period fatigue + an abstract question (DISC-2) is a common shutdown trigger. If she's already drained from SR-1, give her permission to skip DISC-2 and go straight to DISC-3 — which is more concrete (benefits of multiple communities) and lets her end the lesson on a verbal-strength high note. [Goal 1; Present Level: strengths]
